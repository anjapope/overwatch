OVERWATCH

Governing Charter

Project: Overwatch
System: Mayday Network
Status: Foundational Architecture
Purpose: Governing vision and design principles

1. Mission

Overwatch is a persistent open-source intelligence collection, monitoring, analysis, and visualization system designed to transform continuously changing public information into traceable, structured, longitudinal intelligence.

Overwatch will collect information from public sources, preserve the underlying evidence, identify events and entities, detect meaningful changes and relationships, support human analysis, and produce evidence-backed intelligence products.

Its principal analytical domains include, but are not limited to:

geopolitical and security developments;

armed conflict and conflict-zone monitoring;

diplomacy and international relations;

civil unrest and political instability;

economic and market developments;

corporations and industries;

mergers, acquisitions, and corporate policy;

regulation and sanctions;

supply chains, shipping, energy, and infrastructure;

emerging technologies;

humanitarian and disaster events.

Overwatch is intended to become a general-purpose intelligence environment rather than a news aggregator or single-purpose monitoring application.

2. The World Is the Interface

The primary interface of Overwatch is an interactive three-dimensional representation of Earth.

When Overwatch launches, the user sees the globe.

The globe is not a decorative dashboard component. It is the principal operational surface through which intelligence is explored.

The user must be able to:

rotate the Earth through 360 degrees;

zoom continuously between global and regional perspectives;

highlight geographic areas through pointer interaction;

select countries, regions, and eventually finer geographic units;

identify active intelligence hotspots;

select event clusters and individual events;

move through intelligence temporally;

filter the world through analytical layers;

open contextual information without unnecessarily leaving the globe.

Information should remain spatially grounded whenever geography is meaningful.

3. Hotspots

Overwatch will visually identify geographic areas experiencing meaningful activity.

A hotspot is not synonymous with a news article.

Hotspots represent analytical objects derived from one or more underlying events, claims, observations, or indicators.

Hotspots may be generated by:

significant events;

unusual reporting volume;

changes from historical baseline;

watchlist matches;

emerging patterns;

cross-domain convergence;

high-confidence alerts;

analyst-defined conditions.

Hotspot presentation may encode dimensions such as:

significance;

confidence;

recency;

category;

event density;

rate of change.

The interface must avoid equating media volume with real-world significance.

4. Operational Interface

A persistent toolbar will provide access to major analytical functions while preserving the globe as the primary workspace.

Anticipated capabilities include:

Search — entities, places, events, sources, and concepts.

Dossiers — accumulated intelligence concerning countries, organizations, companies, people, industries, locations, and other entities.

Events — chronological and analytical event streams.

Watchlists — persistent intelligence requirements and monitored subjects.

Alerts — significant changes requiring analyst attention.

Briefings — generated and analyst-curated intelligence products.

Sources — access to the evidence underlying Overwatch assessments.

Layers — configurable geographic and analytical overlays.

These capabilities should operate as perspectives upon the shared intelligence environment rather than isolated applications.

5. Temporal Intelligence

Overwatch must preserve historical state.

Events are not disposable notifications.

The user should eventually be able to move backward through time and observe how the information environment changed.

A temporal interface should support:

current state;

previous hours;

previous days;

previous weeks;

previous months;

custom periods;

event evolution;

historical comparison.

Overwatch should ultimately allow an analyst to reconstruct the development of a crisis, conflict, corporate event, political development, or other intelligence subject.

6. Core Intelligence Model

Overwatch will distinguish among several classes of information.

At minimum, the system must conceptually distinguish:

Source — the original material from which information was obtained.

Observation — something directly observed in a source or dataset.

Claim — an assertion made by a source or actor.

Event Candidate — a possible real-world event inferred from reporting.

Event — a structured representation of a real-world occurrence supported by available evidence.

Entity — a persistent person, organization, country, company, location, asset, institution, industry, or other identifiable object.

Relationship — a structured connection between entities.

Indicator — an observation relevant to a standing analytical requirement.

Assessment — an analytical conclusion derived from evidence.

Hypothesis — an unresolved explanatory possibility.

Watch — a persistent intelligence requirement.

Alert — a change or condition meeting defined criteria for analyst attention.

These distinctions must survive implementation.

7. Evidence and Provenance

Overwatch must be evidence-first.

Every substantive analytical conclusion should be traceable downward toward its supporting evidence.

The intended chain is:

Assessment
→ Events / Indicators
→ Claims / Observations
→ Sources
→ Original material

The system must preserve source provenance wherever technically possible.

Overwatch must not silently transform AI-generated interpretation into factual evidence.

Machine-generated conclusions must remain distinguishable from source-derived observations.

8. Epistemic Status

Overwatch will explicitly represent uncertainty.

The system should distinguish among:

directly observed information;

source-reported claims;

independently corroborated reporting;

structured inference;

machine-generated inference;

analyst assessment;

unresolved hypothesis.

Confidence should not be represented as false precision.

Where sources disagree, disagreement should be preserved rather than automatically collapsed into a single narrative.

Source repetition must not automatically count as independent corroboration.

Where multiple reports derive from the same underlying source, Overwatch should attempt to represent that dependency.

9. Intelligence Rather Than Aggregation

Overwatch is not intended to answer:

What are people publishing?

Its higher-level objective is to help answer:

What appears to be happening?

and eventually:

What is changing, why might it matter, what evidence supports that interpretation, and what should be watched next?

Collection is therefore only the first stage of the system.

The intended analytical pipeline is:

COLLECT
→ PRESERVE
→ NORMALIZE
→ EXTRACT
→ GEOLOCATE
→ RESOLVE ENTITIES
→ IDENTIFY CLAIMS
→ CLUSTER REPORTING
→ CORROBORATE
→ IDENTIFY EVENTS
→ DETECT CHANGE
→ ANALYZE
→ ALERT
→ BRIEF
→ ARCHIVE

10. Persistent Analytical Memory

Overwatch must maintain knowledge across time.

New information should be interpreted in relation to previously observed information.

The system should eventually recognize longitudinal relationships such as:

repeated activity;

escalation or de-escalation;

abnormal changes from baseline;

recurring actors;

changes in organizational behavior;

corporate strategic shifts;

regulatory trajectories;

military deployments;

diplomatic sequences;

supply-chain disruptions;

market reactions;

relationships between developments across domains.

Overwatch should become more analytically useful as its evidence base grows.

11. Cross-Domain Intelligence

Overwatch should support relationships among domains rather than isolating them.

For example:

military activity

shipping disruption

government statements

commodity movement

sanctions activity

may collectively be more significant than any individual indicator.

Likewise:

executive turnover

revised guidance

regulatory activity

competitor investment

unusual market movement

may represent an emerging corporate development.

The system should ultimately support detection of such convergences while preserving the evidence underlying them.

12. Watch System

Users must be able to establish persistent intelligence requirements.

Potential watch targets include:

countries;

regions;

conflicts;

companies;

people;

organizations;

industries;

commodities;

infrastructure;

ports;

technologies;

policies;

regulatory subjects;

user-defined analytical questions.

A watch should define what Overwatch is being asked to notice rather than merely what keywords it should search.

13. Human Analytical Authority

Overwatch assists analysis; it does not replace the analyst.

Automation may:

collect;

classify;

extract;

compare;

cluster;

identify anomalies;

propose relationships;

generate summaries;

suggest hypotheses;

draft assessments.

The system must preserve mechanisms for human review, correction, annotation, confirmation, rejection, and reinterpretation.

Analytical provenance should include both machine and human contributions.

14. AI Architecture

Overwatch will not be designed as one monolithic AI agent.

AI capabilities should operate through bounded services or workers with identifiable responsibilities.

Potential workers include:

collection workers;

document-processing workers;

entity extraction workers;

geolocation workers;

claim extraction workers;

event clustering workers;

corroboration workers;

anomaly detection workers;

watch workers;

analytical workers;

briefing workers.

The database and evidence model—not an AI model's transient context—constitute Overwatch's institutional memory.

15. Relationship to Research Studio

Overwatch and Research Studio are sibling systems within the Mayday ecosystem.

They should share infrastructure where useful without becoming the same application.

Research Studio is primarily researcher-driven:

The researcher possesses or acquires material and investigates it.

Overwatch is primarily event-driven:

The external information environment changes and the system detects, preserves, and analyzes those changes.

Information discovered through Overwatch should be transferable into Research Studio while preserving provenance and contextual relationships.

Research Studio should eventually be capable of conducting deeper investigation into subjects discovered through Overwatch.

16. Mayday Architecture

Overwatch must function on a single computer.

Distributed Mayday infrastructure should enhance capability rather than constitute a basic dependency.

As the system develops, Mayday machines may perform specialized background work such as:

collection;

document processing;

indexing;

transcription;

model inference;

scheduled monitoring;

analytical jobs;

archive maintenance.

Jobs should eventually be portable across compatible Mayday workers.

17. Local-First Architecture

Where practical, Overwatch should preserve local ownership of:

collected source material;

structured intelligence;

analyst annotations;

watchlists;

dossiers;

historical state;

derived analytical products.

External APIs and cloud services may extend the system, but core intellectual assets should not depend unnecessarily upon a single external vendor.

18. Extensibility

Early implementation must not artificially constrain the long-term system.

Overwatch should be designed so that additional source types, analytical domains, visualization layers, models, workers, and intelligence methodologies can be added without rewriting the fundamental architecture.

Initial simplicity is acceptable.

Architectural dead ends created solely to produce a minimal demonstration are not.

19. Initial Development Strategy

Development will proceed from interface contract toward intelligence capability.

The initial development sequence is:

Establish governing architecture.

Build the interactive globe.

Define structured event fixtures.

Display simulated intelligence on the globe.

Implement geographic selection.

Implement event inspection.

Implement temporal filtering.

Implement analytical layers.

Establish persistent local intelligence storage.

Introduce controlled real-world collection.

Establish provenance and source preservation.

Introduce entity and claim extraction.

Introduce event clustering and corroboration.

Introduce watch capabilities.

Introduce alerts and briefing.

Develop longitudinal and cross-domain analysis.

This sequence provides visible functionality early without allowing superficial demonstrations to dictate the eventual architecture.

20. Initial Milestone

The first operational milestone is:

Launch Overwatch. Earth appears. The user can rotate and zoom the globe. Structured test events appear as geographic hotspots. Countries and hotspots can be selected, and their underlying information can be inspected.

This milestone establishes the basic interaction contract upon which subsequent intelligence capability will be built.

21. Governing Principle

Overwatch should make an increasingly complex information environment intelligible without concealing the evidence, uncertainty, disagreement, or analytical reasoning upon which that intelligibility depends.

The world is the interface.
Evidence is the foundation.
Time is part of the data.
Analysis remains traceable.
